Note: Cause A reproduces the ACTUAL production scheme -- an independent
backward solve from tau=0 to tau=1/52 with the calibration pricer's fixed
N_tau=10 CN steps (dt = (1/52)/10), matching make_cached_pricer exactly.
Cause B instead diagnoses a HYPOTHETICAL single shared solve to tau=1.0
with that same global Nt=10, to test whether checkpointing off a long
solve (rather than solving each maturity independently) would explain
the bad 1w accuracy. It is independent of, and uses a different time
horizon than, Cause A.

Neither (A) nor (B) explains the magnitude of the 1w error on any date:
Rannacher leaves the answer essentially unchanged (ratio ~1.0), and the
error does not refine at O(dt^2) even once the mesh clears the
steps_to_1wk>=1 threshold. The 'tau=0 err' column above shows why: the
error is already present in the L2-projected payoff BEFORE any time
stepping -- evaluation points for near-ATM strikes land within <1 grid
cell of the payoff kink (see 'distance from the kink' above), where the
smooth B-spline projection of the kinked payoff itself deviates sharply
from the true intrinsic value. This is a SPATIAL under-resolution bug
(scales ~O(h); halving h roughly halves it -- verified separately at
N=128/256), not a time-stepping bug -- consistent with the task's
'third bug' scenario for cause (B).

Fix attempted: replace the L2-projected payoff with a Schoenberg /
variation-diminishing (Greville-point) projection -- c_k = sqrt(h)*
payoff(xi_k), xi_k = centre of basis k's support. This reproduces the
payoff exactly away from the kink (it is locally affine there) and is
provably non-oscillatory, so it ELIMINATES THE NEGATIVE-PRICE ARTIFACT
on every date that had one (2020-01-15, 2021-09-15) -- a real, verified
fix for the most obviously broken symptom (arbitrage violation).
However, it does NOT reduce the overall ||err||_inf vs COS at tau=1/52
(in fact it slightly increases it here): a wider scan around the kink
shows Greville trades L2's small, fast-decaying oscillation for a
LARGER, smooth overshoot concentrated within ~1 grid cell of the kink
(both methods are convex combinations of payoff samples roughly h apart;
h*S0 ~ 1-2% of spot here, so even an oscillation-free blend is coarse
relative to the option's notional). This confirms the diagnosis from
the previous run: the dominant error is spatial under-resolution (h too
large relative to the kink), not a projection-algorithm defect -- no
change of projection METHOD on this grid fixes the overall magnitude;
only refining N (or true local mesh refinement at the kink, not
attempted here since it conflicts with the uniform-grid Toeplitz
machinery used elsewhere) reduces it, per the N=64/128/256 sweep above.
