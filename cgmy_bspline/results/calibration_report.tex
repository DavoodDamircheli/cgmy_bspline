\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}
% =============================================================================

This report documents the end-to-end market calibration of the
Carr--G\'{e}man--Madan--Yor (CGMY) tempered stable process to
S\&P~500 (SPX) implied-volatility (IV) surfaces observed on three
snapshot dates spanning markedly different market regimes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{2020-01-15} — low-volatility, pre-COVID environment
        (VIX $\approx 13$).
  \item \textbf{2020-03-18} — acute COVID-19 crisis
        (VIX $\approx 76$, largest single-day SPX declines on record).
  \item \textbf{2021-09-15} — post-COVID recovery
        (VIX $\approx 20$).
\end{itemize}

The calibration exploits the B-Spline Galerkin + Crank--Nicolson (CN)
solver developed in the preceding sections of this work
as its inner pricing engine.  No external option-pricing library is used.
Optimisation is performed with a two-phase scheme (Differential
Evolution followed by L-BFGS-B) over an unconstrained reparametrisation
of the CGMY parameter space.

% =============================================================================
\section{The CGMY Model}
\label{sec:model}
% =============================================================================

The CGMY L\'{e}vy process has characteristic exponent
\begin{equation}
  \Psi(u) = C\,\Gamma(-Y)\bigl[(M - iu)^{Y} - M^{Y}
             + (G + iu)^{Y} - G^{Y}\bigr],
  \label{eq:cgmy_cf}
\end{equation}
where $C > 0$ (overall jump intensity), $G > 0$ (left-tail decay),
$M > 1$ (right-tail decay), $Y \in (0,2)\setminus\{1\}$ (fine-structure
index).  For $Y \in (1,2)$ the process has infinite variation and is the
dominant regime for equity markets.

Under the risk-neutral measure the log-price dynamics are
\[
  \log(S_T / S_0) = (r - q - \omega_S)\,T + X_T,
\]
where $X_T$ is a centred CGMY process and
$\omega_S = C\,\Gamma(-Y)\bigl[(M-1)^Y - M^Y + (G+1)^Y - G^Y\bigr]$
is the martingale correction.

\section{Data and Surface Construction}
\label{sec:data}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Raw data}

Raw option chains are sourced from OptionMetrics for three snapshot
dates.  Each chain contains end-of-day bid, ask, and implied volatility
for all listed SPX calls and puts.

\subsection{Quality filtering (Step~2)}

The following filters are applied to each raw chain before any
further processing:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Remove quotes with zero bid, zero open interest, or
        non-positive mid-price.
  \item Remove quotes with negative implied volatility.
  \item Remove quotes with bid $>$ ask (crossed markets).
  \item Compute mid-price $= \tfrac{1}{2}(\text{bid} + \text{ask})$ and
        relative spread $= (\text{ask} - \text{bid}) / \text{mid}$.
\end{enumerate}

Two spread-filter thresholds are considered:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Primary (05):} $\text{rel\_spread} < 5\%$.
  \item \textbf{Relaxed (15):} $\text{rel\_spread} < 15\%$.
\end{itemize}
The primary filter is used for all main results; the relaxed filter is
used for the crisis-date robustness check (\S\ref{sec:robustness}).

\subsection{Maturity selection (Step~3)}

Five target maturities (in calendar days) are selected from each chain:
7, 30, 93, 182, and 366 days.  The actual expiry closest to each target
is chosen.

\subsection{Forward and discount factor calibration (Step~4)}

For each selected maturity, the forward price $F$ and discount factor
$D = e^{-rT}$ are extracted by fitting put-call parity via OLS
regression on paired strike prices.  Concretely, writing
$C_{\mathrm{mid}}(K) - P_{\mathrm{mid}}(K) = F\,D - D\,K$
gives the linear model
\[
  y_i = a - D\,K_i, \qquad
  y_i = C_{\mathrm{mid}}(K_i) - P_{\mathrm{mid}}(K_i),
\]
from which $D = -\hat{\beta}_1$ and $F = \hat{\beta}_0 / D$.
This ensures the model forward price exactly matches the market-implied
forward, a necessary condition for arbitrage-free pricing.
Per-maturity effective rates are then
\[
  r_{\text{eff}}(T) = -\ln D / T, \qquad
  q_{\text{eff}}(T) = r_{\text{eff}}(T) - \ln(F/S_0)/T,
\]
where the spot proxy $S_0 = F_{\min}\,D_{\min}$ is computed from
the shortest-dated maturity.

\subsection{OTM selection and moneyness filter (Steps~5--6)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OTM rule:} retain puts for $K < F$, calls for $K \geq F$.
  \item \textbf{Moneyness filter:} retain strikes with
        $\kappa = K/F \in [0.85, 1.15]$.
\end{itemize}
The resulting quote counts per date and spread threshold are summarised
in Table~\ref{tab:quote_counts}.

\begin{table}[ht]

\label{tab:quote_counts}

\section{Pricing Methodology}
\label{sec:pricing}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{B-Spline Galerkin inner pricer}

European put prices are computed by solving the CGMY fractional
backward FPDE on a log-price spatial domain $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}]$
with cubic ($p=3$) cardinal B-spline basis functions and
Crank--Nicolson ($N_\tau$ time steps) time-stepping.
The spatial semi-discretisation produces the linear ODE system
\[
  M_h\,\dot{c}(\tau) + A_h\,c(\tau) = f(\tau),
\]
where $M_h$ is the B-spline mass matrix, $A_h$ is the CGMY
operator matrix (sum of a first-order drift term, a Toeplitz-structured
fractional stiffness matrix, and a kill-rate term), and $c(\tau)$ is
the coefficient vector.
The CN scheme advances this system from $\tau = 0$ (expiry payoff) to
$\tau = T$ (today).

\subsection{Domain and grid}

The default \texttt{make\_grid} in the B-spline package centres the
spatial domain at $\log K$ with width proportional to $1/G + 1/M$,
which collapses to only $\pm 0.1$ log-units for $G = M = 10$ — too
narrow for active CGMY.  For calibration a fixed-width grid is used
instead:
\[
  [x_{\min}, x_{\max}] = [\log K_{\text{ref}} - 2,\;
                           \log K_{\text{ref}} + 2],
\]
covering $S \in [K_{\text{ref}}\,e^{-2},\, K_{\text{ref}}\,e^{2}]$,
a ratio of approximately $1\!:\!55$ around the reference strike.
Benchmark tests against the COS pricer confirm relative pricing errors
below 0.25\% at $N = 256$ under this domain choice.

\subsection{Homogeneity pricing of a strike strip}

Rather than solving one PDE per strike, the homogeneity identity
\[
  P(S_0, K; r, q, T) = \frac{K}{K_{\text{ref}}}\,
  P\!\left(S_0\,e^{-qT}\cdot\frac{K_{\text{ref}}}{K},\;
           K_{\text{ref}};\; r, q{=}0, T\right)
\]
is used to price all strikes $K_j$ in a single PDE solve with
reference strike $K_{\text{ref}} = S_0$.  The dividend yield $q$ is
absorbed into the effective spot
$S_0^{\mathrm{eff}} = S_0\,e^{-qT}$, which shifts the evaluation
point inside the already-computed solution.
The evaluation points are
\[
  x_j = \log S_0^{\mathrm{eff}} + \log K_{\text{ref}} - \log K_j,
\]
and put-call parity yields call prices when $K_j \geq F$.
This reduces the per-maturity pricing cost from $O(n_K)$ PDE solves
to a \emph{single} solve plus $n_K$ B-spline evaluations.

\subsection{Caching of CGMY-independent matrices}

Two matrices dominate the assembly cost:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $M_h$ (mass matrix): computed via \texttt{scipy.integrate.quad}
        numerical integration over B-spline products; cost $\approx
        490\,\mathrm{ms}$ at $N=64$; depends only on $(N, p, h,
        x_{\min})$ — \emph{independent of CGMY parameters}.
  \item $D_h$ (derivative matrix): similarly $\approx 480\,\mathrm{ms}$;
        also CGMY-independent.
  \item $K_G, K_M$ (fractional stiffness matrices): computed via IFFT
        of tempered coefficients; cost $\approx 5\,\mathrm{ms}$;
        \emph{depends on $(G, M, Y)$}.
\end{itemize}

The factory function \texttt{make\_cached\_pricer} pre-builds $M_h$
and $D_h$ \emph{once} per maturity at calibration start, then returns
a closure that only recomputes $K_G, K_M$ per optimizer call.
This yields a $\sim\!100\times$ speedup: a full 5-maturity surface
evaluation drops from $\approx 4.9\,\mathrm{s}$ to
$\approx 51\,\mathrm{ms}$, making the two-phase calibration
tractable within minutes.

\section{Calibration Methodology}
\label{sec:calibration}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Parameter space and unconstrained transform}

The physical parameter bounds are
\[
  C \in [10^{-3}, 20],\quad
  G \in [0.1, 80],\quad
  M \in [1.01, 80],\quad
  Y \in [1.05, 1.95].
\]
To convert a constrained problem into an unconstrained one the
bijective transform
\begin{equation}
  \eta_1 = \log C, \quad
  \eta_2 = \log G, \quad
  \eta_3 = \log(M-1), \quad
  \eta_4 = \log\!\left(\frac{Y-1}{2-Y}\right)
  \label{eq:transform}
\end{equation}
is used, with inverse
\[
  C = e^{\eta_1},\quad G = e^{\eta_2},\quad
  M = 1 + e^{\eta_3},\quad
  Y = \frac{2\,e^{\eta_4} + 1}{1 + e^{\eta_4}}.
\]
The bounds on $\eta$ are derived programmatically from the physical
bounds (never guessed by hand), so any change to the physical bounds
propagates automatically.  The resulting $\eta$-space bounds are
$\eta_1 \in [-6.91, 3.00]$,
$\eta_2 \in [-2.30, 4.38]$,
$\eta_3 \in [-4.61, 4.37]$,
$\eta_4 \in [-2.94, 2.94]$.

\subsection{Objective function}

The calibration minimises the vega-normalised mean-squared price error
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\eta) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{quotes}}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{quotes}}}
  \left(\frac{V_{\text{model},i}(\eta) - V_{\text{mid},i}}
             {\max(\nu_i,\, \nu_{\text{floor}})}\right)^{\!2},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $V_{\text{model},i}$ is the model OTM price, $V_{\text{mid},i}$
is the market mid-price, $\nu_i$ is the Black--Scholes forward vega,
and $\nu_{\text{floor}} = 10^{-3}$ prevents blow-up for deep OTM
quotes with negligible sensitivity.

Dividing by vega converts the objective from a price-error MSE to an
approximate IV-error MSE, so that near-ATM liquid quotes
(high vega) and deep-OTM quotes (low vega) contribute roughly
equally in implied-volatility units.

\subsection{Two-phase optimisation}

\paragraph{Phase~1: Differential Evolution (global search).}
Scipy's \texttt{differential\_evolution} is run with a coarse $N=32$,
$N_\tau = 5$ cached pricer (sufficient for identifying the basin
of attraction; $\approx 1.5\%$ price error relative to $N=256$).
Fixed settings (used for all dates, for reproducibility):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{seed} $= 0$
  \item \texttt{maxiter} $= 300$ (early stopping: \texttt{tol} $= 10^{-7}$)
  \item \texttt{popsize} $= 15$ (population $= 15 \times 4 = 60$ individuals)
  \item \texttt{polish} $=$ \texttt{False}
  \item \texttt{mutation} $= (0.5,\,1.5)$,\; \texttt{recombination} $= 0.7$
\end{itemize}
At $\approx 25\,\mathrm{ms}$ per evaluation the maximum budget of
$18{,}060$ function calls would take $\approx 7.5$ minutes; in
practice DE converged in 9\,000--11\,000 calls ($\approx 4$ minutes).

\paragraph{Phase~2: L-BFGS-B (local refinement).}
Starting from the DE solution, a high-accuracy $N=64$, $N_\tau=10$
cached pricer is substituted and \texttt{scipy.optimize.minimize}
with \texttt{method='L-BFGS-B'} is run to convergence
(\texttt{ftol} $= 10^{-12}$, \texttt{gtol} $= 10^{-8}$).
At $\approx 51\,\mathrm{ms}$ per evaluation, 130--265 calls
are required ($10\text{--}14\,\mathrm{s}$).

\subsection{Diagnostics}

After Phase~2, every OTM market quote is re-priced with the
$N=64$ pricer and the model price inverted to model implied
volatility via Brent's method on the Black--1976 forward formula.
The IV RMSE and maximum absolute IV error across all quotes are
reported as calibration quality metrics.

\section{Crisis-Date Robustness Check}
\label{sec:robustness}
% =============================================================================

The data-roadmap (Section~5) flags the 2020-03-18 date as requiring a
robustness test: does the calibrated parameter vector change materially
when the spread filter is relaxed from 5\% to 15\%?
Table~\ref{tab:robustness} reports the absolute and relative differences.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Crisis-date (2020-03-18) parameter robustness: spread05 vs spread15.}
\label{tab:robustness}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Parameter & Spread 5\% & Spread 15\% & $|\Delta|$ & $|\Delta|/|{\cdot}|_{\text{05}}$ \\
\midrule
$C^*$ & 0.5431 & 0.5462 & 0.003 &  0.6\% \\
$G^*$ & 0.100  & 0.100  & 0.000 &  0.0\% \\
$M^*$ & 27.79  & 6.65   & 21.14 & 76.1\% \\
$Y^*$ & 1.050  & 1.050  & 0.000 &  0.0\% \\
\midrule
IV RMSE & 7.19\,vp & 11.05\,vp & 3.87\,vp & 53.8\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Finding.}
$C^*$, $G^*$, and $Y^*$ are highly stable (changes $\leq 1\%$) under
the relaxed spread filter.  However, $M^*$ shifts dramatically from
$27.8$ to $6.7$ — a $76\%$ reduction.  This instability has a clear
structural explanation: $M$ controls the exponential decay rate of the
right tail of the Lévy measure (upward jumps), and is therefore
identified primarily by long-maturity OTM call options.  The 5\% filter
retains \emph{no} maturities beyond 93\,d; the 15\% filter recovers 5
maturities including 182\,d and 366\,d calls.  With dramatically
different surface shapes entering the objective, the optimizer finds a
different $M^*$ while holding the other three parameters near-constant.

The substantially worse RMSE under the 15\% filter (11.05~vp vs
7.19~vp) indicates that the additional quotes admitted by the looser
filter are noisy (wide spreads, stale quotes) and make the fitting
problem harder, not easier.  The 5\% surface, though sparse, is more
internally consistent.

For the paper's Section~7 the 5\%-filter result is reported as the
primary finding.  The 15\%-filter result is documented here as
evidence that \emph{crisis-date $M^*$ should be interpreted with
caution}: it is sensitive to which maturities survive the quality
filter, and both estimates press against the feasible-set boundary
(lower bound $G=0.1$, lower bound $Y=1.05$), suggesting the data
do not uniquely identify the full CGMY parameter vector in this regime.

\section{Financial Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Jump-intensity scaling with market regime}

The most striking cross-date pattern is the $C^*$ parameter:
\[
  C^*_{\text{low-vol}} = 0.049,\quad
  C^*_{\text{crisis}}  = 0.543,\quad
  C^*_{\text{recovery}}= 0.097.
\]
The crisis value is \textbf{$5.6\times$ larger} than the recovery value
and \textbf{$11\times$ larger} than the low-vol value.  Since $C$ scales
the overall intensity of the CGMY Lévy measure (more precisely, $C$
multiplies the jump rate density at every size), a larger $C^*$ means
the risk-neutral measure assigns a substantially higher probability to
large price moves.  This is consistent with the known behaviour of
risk-neutral CGMY parameters during stress periods: market participants
price options as if the world is in a higher-activity jump regime,
even after accounting for the elevated level of the IVS through
the volatility-normalised objective.

\subsection{Fine-structure index stability}

The $Y^*$ estimates are
\[
  Y^*_{\text{low-vol}} = 1.089,\quad
  Y^*_{\text{crisis}} = 1.050,\quad
  Y^*_{\text{recovery}} = 1.050.
\]
The cross-regime range is $0.039$ — a spread of only \textbf{3.6\%}
relative to the cross-date mean $\bar{Y} = 1.063$.
This narrow range is consistent with the theoretical interpretation of
$Y$ as a structural fine-structure index governing the activity of
small jumps, which changes slowly with market conditions and is
largely regime-independent.  All three estimates are in the
infinite-variation regime $Y \in (1,2)$, as expected for SPX options.

\paragraph{Boundary note.}
In two of the three dates ($Y^* = 1.050$) the optimizer converges
to the lower bound of the $Y$ search domain $[1.05, 1.95]$.  This
suggests the data would prefer $Y$ slightly below 1.05.  The bound
is imposed by the theoretical requirement $Y > 1$ for infinite variation
plus a numerical margin; the proximity to the boundary means $Y^*$
should be interpreted as an upper-bound constraint rather than a
well-identified interior minimum.

\subsection{Tail-decay parameters}

The $G^*$ and $M^*$ estimates reveal an interesting asymmetry:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Non-crisis dates:} $G^* \approx 2.2\text{--}2.6$,
        $M^* = 80$ (upper bound).  A small $G$ (slow left-tail decay)
        implies a heavier downside tail — consistent with the well-known
        negative skewness of SPX risk-neutral distributions.
        $M^*$ hitting the upper bound means the optimizer cannot
        distinguish $M = 80$ from $M = \infty$: the data are
        insensitive to the right-tail decay rate, likely because the
        $\kappa \in [0.85, 1.15]$ moneyness window contains few
        deep OTM calls.

  \item \textbf{Crisis date (5\% filter):} $G^* = 0.10$ (lower bound),
        $M^* = 27.8$.  During the crisis \emph{both} tails are
        affected, but the optimizer reaches the $G$ lower bound,
        indicating a very heavy left tail that cannot be distinguished
        from an even heavier one within the search domain.  $M^*$
        falls substantially (from 80 to 27.8), reflecting the market
        pricing some right-tail risk (large upward moves during
        crisis bear-market rallies).
\end{itemize}

\section{Crisis-Date Fit Caveat}
\label{sec:crisis_caveat}
% =============================================================================

The 2020-03-18 calibration (5\%-filter) yields an IV RMSE of
\textbf{7.19~vol-points}, well above the 3~vp acceptance threshold
met on the other two dates.  This is not a failure of the calibration
algorithm; it reflects two structural issues with the crisis surface:

\paragraph{(i) Sparse, two-maturity surface.}
The 5\% spread filter retains only 113 quotes across the 30\,d and
93\,d maturities.  Fitting a four-parameter model to a two-maturity
surface is underdetermined: $C$ and $Y$ are primarily identified by
the level and curvature of the smile, while $G$ and $M$ are primarily
identified by the skew \emph{across maturities} — information that
requires at least three maturities.

\paragraph{(ii) Extremely elevated IV level.}
SPX ATM implied volatility on 2020-03-18 was approximately 80\%.
The worst-fit quotes are all near-ATM 30\,d options
(log-moneyness $\approx 0$, IV $\approx 0.80$), where the model
underestimates IV by up to 17~vp.  The CGMY process, calibrated to
match the overall skew shape, cannot simultaneously match the extreme
level without distorting the smile curvature.  This is a known
limitation of parametric Lévy models: they have limited flexibility to
jointly match the level and curvature of the smile during crisis events.

\paragraph{(iii) Boundary convergence.}
The optimizer converges to $G^* = 0.10$ (lower bound) and $Y^* = 1.05$
(lower bound), indicating the loss surface does not have a well-defined
interior minimum under these data.  The reported parameter values are
therefore constrained estimates rather than interior maximum-likelihood
solutions.

\paragraph{Recommendation for Section~7 paper text.}
Report the crisis calibration result with the following caveat:
``\textit{The 2020-03-18 surface, reduced to two maturities by the
5\% spread filter, yields an in-sample IV RMSE of 7.2~vol-points.
We attribute this to the extreme market conditions during the COVID-19
crash rather than to a deficiency of the calibration procedure:
the sparse surface is insufficient to identify all four CGMY parameters,
and the model is asked to match implied volatilities near 80\%,
a regime outside the domain covered by typical CGMY calibrations.
The crisis result is reported for completeness; the low-volatility
and recovery dates, where the surface covers five maturities and
the model is well-identified, are the primary evidence for the
calibration methodology.}''

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

The B-Spline Galerkin + CN solver serves as an effective inner
pricer for market calibration of the CGMY model to SPX option surfaces.
The full pipeline — surface construction, forward/discount calibration,
OTM selection, cached pricing, two-phase optimisation — runs in under
5~minutes per date on a single CPU core.

Key results:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Non-crisis dates:} IV RMSE of 1.97~vp (2021-09-15) and
        2.47~vp (2020-01-15), comfortably within the 3~vp target.
  \item \textbf{Crisis date:} IV RMSE of 7.19~vp due to a sparse
        two-maturity surface and extreme IV levels; this is a data
        limitation, not an algorithmic failure.
  \item \textbf{Jump intensity} $C^*$ is 5.6--11$\times$ elevated at
        the crisis date relative to the non-crisis dates,
        consistent with the risk-neutral measure implying a higher-activity
        jump regime during stress.
  \item \textbf{Fine-structure index} $Y^*$ is stable across regimes
        (range 3.6\% of mean), consistent with its theoretical
        interpretation as a structural parameter.
  \item \textbf{Crisis robustness:} $C^*$, $G^*$, $Y^*$ are stable
        ($<1\%$ change) under the 15\%-spread filter; $M^*$ shifts
        by 76\%, reflecting the dependence of right-tail identification
        on long-maturity quote availability.
\end{itemize}

The cached-pricer architecture (pre-built $M_h$, $D_h$ per maturity;
IFFT-only rebuild per optimizer call) is the key computational
innovation enabling this calibration throughput.  Without caching,
a single 5-maturity loss evaluation would require $\approx 5$~s;
with caching it requires $\approx 51$~ms — a $100\times$ speedup that
transforms a 9-hour brute-force calibration into a 4-minute workflow.
